Building Alignment

← Back to NexoNest

Overview

Building Alignment explores how the positional arrangement of buildings within a block affects energy performance. The study introduces a quantifiable alignment metric and analyzes its influence on heating, cooling, and primary energy demand across urban layouts.

Using parametric models of a 16-building prototype block in Tehran’s ASHRAE 3B climate, the research systematically varies alignment along X, Y, and XY axes. Results demonstrate that subtle geometric misalignments can shift block-level energy performance by up to 2–3%, underscoring the energy relevance of spatial organization itself.

Methodology

Prototype Block

16-building residential prototype (15×15 m footprint, 7 floors ≈ 22.4 m) typical of Tehran’s ASHRAE 3B climate. Materials, envelope, and occupancy schedules held constant to isolate geometry.

Inter-building distances were derived from a calibrated Shadow Impact Factor (SIF): a height multiplier indicating where neighboring mass starts meaningfully reducing solar access. The SIF curve flattens beyond ~2.5–3 × height, which we adopted as the base spacing for all alignment tests.

Alignment Definition

Previous definitions of alignment was highly related to computer graphics and works with point and line definitions. As result, we need to define alignment based on them but in a way it's applicable to the context of urban design and buildings.

Alignment is the normalized overlap ratio between consecutive buildings (1.0 perfectly aligned, 0.0 fully staggered). Three families were generated: X-alignment, Y-alignment, and XY-alignment, each tested with uniform and non-uniform distributions.

Simulation Workflow

  • Parametric generation in Rhino / Grasshopper (Python) with batch layout creation and duplicate filtering.
  • Energy modeling via Ladybug Tools, OpenStudio, and EnergyPlus.
  • Outputs aggregated as Heating, Cooling, Thermal Load, and Primary Energy Intensity.

Workflow: SIF-based spacing → layout families → energy simulation and aggregation.

Shadow Impact Factor

To ground alignment in environmental relevance, the Shadow Impact Factor quantifies how inter-building distance modulates incident radiation. For Tehran’s mid-rise typology, distances between 2–3× building height capture the critical range where radiation loss plateaus, defining a baseline spacing for the study.

This factor ensures geometric changes remain climatically meaningful.

Window-to-Wall Ratio Optimization

Objective Sweep

A multi-objective WWR optimization was conducted (5–95% step 5%) targeting minimum heating/cooling loads and ASE, with maximum sDA and View.

Optimal Ratios

  • North – 20%
  • South – 25%
  • East – 20%
  • West – 15%

Assumptions

All simulations used ASHRAE 90.1-2019 envelope definitions and neutralized HVAC efficiencies (COP=1) to isolate geometric and alignment effects on thermal performance.

Key Findings

X-Alignment

Lower alignment increases all load types. Alignment magnitude influences energy more strongly than geometric distribution pattern.

Y-Alignment

Cooling and primary energy outcomes depend heavily on the regularity of building distribution. Non-uniform layouts often outperform uniform grids.

XY-Alignment

Misalignment in both axes amplifies energy demand. Primary energy correlates linearly with total misalignment percentage.

Urban Implication

Minor geometric offsets—within typical construction tolerances—can influence block-scale energy demand by up to 2.4%. This reveals alignment as a valid urban energy parameter.

Dataset & Validation

400 simulations were performed; 50 were re-run for cross-validation using independent model exports. Deviations stayed below 0.5 kWh/m² for all metrics, confirming reproducibility.

  • Primary energy uses a 3.7 conversion factor (national electricity coefficient).
  • Strong negative correlation between alignment ratio and total loads.
